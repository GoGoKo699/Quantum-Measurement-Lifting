# Learning Quantum Measurement Lifting

**Anchor:** John Watrous, *The Theory of Quantum Information* (Cambridge University Press, 2018).

Begin with the [worked experiment](WORKED_EXAMPLE.md): what enters a destructive detector, what the retained output must do, and why correct probabilities alone are insufficient. The [Watrous bridge](WATROUS_BRIDGE.md) then identifies the exact textbook sections and notation needed for the project.

Follow [M01–M30](MILESTONES.md) in order for a connected route. M01–M12 assemble the background into one operational question. M13–M30 build the construction, connect it to the adaptive converse, and interpret the exactness and resource boundaries. Dependencies identify useful shorter specialist routes without changing the map.

Each milestone states a central question, an understanding target, dependencies, a conceptual connection and canonical source pointers. These are shared scientific targets, not a record of any learner's progress or a personal testing protocol.

The project itself supplies the cofactor construction, finite-slot polynomial argument, Zariski-density and determinant-divisibility steps, and full-rank obstruction. Watrous supports the quantum-information language; it is not presented as a source of the new theorem or a complete algebraic-geometry course. Primary research citations serve attribution, not additional mandatory educational anchors.

For the direct proof route, use the [scientific overview](../science/README.md). For actual finite calculations and their limitations, use [verification](../verification/README.md).
